<header class="entry-header"><h1 class="entry-title">The Meanings of the Runes</h1></header><!-- .entry-header -->
<div class="entry-content">
<figure id="attachment_1645" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-1645" style="width: 179px" class="wp-caption alignright"><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Runestone-Sm_5_Transjö.jpg"><img decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Runestone-Sm_5_Transjö-179x300.jpg" alt="Transjö (SM5) Runestone (photo by Berig)" width="179" height="300" class="size-medium wp-image-1645" srcset="https://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Runestone-Sm_5_Transjö-179x300.jpg 179w, https://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Runestone-Sm_5_Transjö-612x1024.jpg 612w, https://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Runestone-Sm_5_Transjö.jpg 1921w" sizes="(max-width: 179px) 100vw, 179px" /></a><figcaption id="caption-attachment-1645" class="wp-caption-text">Transjö (SM5) Runestone (photo by Berig)</figcaption></figure>
<p>This page is the fourth part of a five-part article on the runes. The other four parts are:</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/runes/">Part I: Introduction</a><br />
<a href="http://norse-mythology.org/runes/the-origins-of-the-runes/">Part II: The Origins of the Runes</a><br />
<a href="http://norse-mythology.org/runes/runic-philosophy-and-magic/">Part III: Runic Philosophy and Magic</a><br />
<a href="http://norse-mythology.org/runes/the-best-books-on-the-runes/">Part V: The 10 Best Books on the Runes</a></p>
<p>This section provides the sign, name, phoneme (sound), and short description of the meaning of each of the twenty-four runes that comprise the Elder Futhark. The given meanings are based on the medieval Rune Poems (which are conveniently available online <a href="https://www.ragweedforge.com/poems.html">here</a>) exclusively. Where our present knowledge isn&#8217;t extensive enough to give an explanation of which one can be reasonably certain, this is noted and the meaning is left unexplained or only partially explained. This article is hardly the place for esoteric speculations, which have been avoided. (If you&#8217;re interested in going beyond the evidence and using less academically acceptable means of discerning other meanings of the runes, you have to do that yourself. The books on my list of <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/runes/the-best-books-on-the-runes/">The 10 Best Books on the Runes</a> might help.)</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Fehu.png"><img decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Fehu.png" alt="" title="Fehu" width="30" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-549" /></a> Name: Fehu, &#8220;cattle.&#8221; Phoneme: F. Meaning: wealth.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Uruz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Uruz.png" alt="" title="Uruz" width="36" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-550" /></a> Name: Uruz, &#8220;aurochs.&#8221; Phoneme: U (long and/or short). Meaning: strength of will.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Thurisaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Thurisaz.png" alt="" title="Thurisaz" width="25" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-552" /></a> Name: Thurisaz, &#8220;<a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/giants/">Giant</a>.&#8221; Phoneme: Th (both soft and hard). Meaning: danger, suffering.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Ansuz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Ansuz.png" alt="" title="Ansuz" width="18" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-553" /></a> Name: Ansuz, &#8220;an <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/the-aesir-gods-and-goddesses/">Aesir</a> god.&#8221; Phoneme: A (long and/or short). Meaning: prosperity, vitality.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Raidho.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Raidho.png" alt="" title="Raidho" width="24" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-556" /></a> Name: Raidho, &#8220;journey on horseback.&#8221; Phoneme: R. Meaning: movement, work, growth.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Kaunan.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Kaunan.png" alt="" title="Kaunan" width="26" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-557" /></a> Name: Kaunan, &#8220;ulcer.&#8221; Phoneme: K. Meaning: mortality, pain.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Gebo.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Gebo.png" alt="" title="Gebo" width="30" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-558" /></a> Name: Gebo, &#8220;gift.&#8221; Phoneme: G. Meaning: generosity.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Wunjo.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Wunjo.png" alt="" title="Wunjo" width="20" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-559" /></a> Name: Wunjo, &#8220;joy.&#8221; Phoneme: W. Meaning: joy, ecstasy.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Hagalaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Hagalaz.png" alt="" title="Hagalaz" width="40" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-560" /></a> Name:Hagalaz, &#8220;hail.&#8221; Phoneme: H. Meaning: destruction, chaos.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Naudhiz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Naudhiz.png" alt="" title="Naudhiz" width="24" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-561" /></a> Name: Naudhiz, &#8220;need.&#8221; Phoneme: N. Meaning: need, unfulfilled desire.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Isa.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Isa.png" alt="" title="Isa" width="4" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-562" /></a> Name: Isaz, &#8220;ice.&#8221; Phoneme: I (long and/or short). Meaning: unknown (the rune poems are ambiguous and contradictory).</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Jera.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Jera.png" alt="" title="Jera" width="38" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-563" /></a> Name: Jera, &#8220;year.&#8221; Phoneme: Germanic J, modern English Y. Meaning: harvest, reward.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Eiwaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Eiwaz.png" alt="" title="Eiwaz" width="36" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-564" /></a> Name: Eihwaz, &#8220;yew.&#8221; Phoneme: I pronounced like &#8220;Eye.&#8221; Meaning: strength, stability.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Pertho.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Pertho.png" alt="" title="Pertho" width="25" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-565" /></a> Name: unknown. Phoneme: P. Meaning: unknown. (Note: the theory that this rune&#8217;s name was &#8220;Pertho&#8221; is just speculation. No one really knows, because the Viking Age and medieval sources are too vague.)</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Algiz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Algiz.png" alt="" title="Algiz" width="40" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-566" /></a> Name: unknown (the rune poems are contradictory). Phoneme: Z. Meaning: protection from enemies, defense of that which one loves.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Sowilo.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Sowilo.png" alt="" title="Sowilo" width="24" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-567" /></a> Name: Sowilo, &#8220;sun.&#8221; Phoneme: S. Meaning: success, solace.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Tiwaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Tiwaz.png" alt="" title="Tiwaz" width="45" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-568" /></a> Name: Tiwaz, &#8220;the god <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/the-aesir-gods-and-goddesses/tyr/">Tiwaz</a>.&#8221; Phoneme: T. Meaning: victory, honor.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Berkanan.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Berkanan.png" alt="" title="Berkanan" width="25" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-569" /></a> Name: Berkanan, &#8220;birch.&#8221; Phoneme: B. Meaning: fertility, growth, sustenance.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Ehwaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Ehwaz.png" alt="" title="Ehwaz" width="40" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-570" /></a> Name: Ehwaz, &#8220;horse.&#8221; Phoneme: E (long and/or short). Meaning: trust, faith, companionship.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Mannaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Mannaz.png" alt="" title="Mannaz" width="40" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-571" /></a> Name: Mannaz, &#8220;man.&#8221; Phoneme: M. Meaning: augmentation, support.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Laguz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Laguz.png" alt="" title="Laguz" width="24" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-572" /></a> Name: Laguz. Phoneme: L. Meaning: formlessness, chaos, potentiality, the unknown.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Ingwaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Ingwaz.png" alt="" title="Ingwaz" width="32" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-573" /></a> Name: Ingwaz, &#8220;the god <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/the-vanir-gods-and-goddesses/freyr/">Ingwaz</a>.&#8221; Phoneme: Ng. Meaning: fertilization, the beginning of something, the actualization of potential.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Othala.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Othala.png" alt="" title="Othala" width="41" height="57" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-574" /></a> Name: Othalan, &#8220;inheritance.&#8221; Phoneme: O (long and/or short). Meaning: inheritance, heritage, tradition, nobility.</p>
<p><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Dagaz.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Dagaz.png" alt="" title="Dagaz" width="40" height="60" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-575" /></a> Name: Dagaz, &#8220;day.&#8221; Phoneme: D. Meaning: hope, happiness.</p>
