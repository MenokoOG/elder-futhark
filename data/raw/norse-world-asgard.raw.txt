<header class="entry-header"><h1 class="entry-title">Asgard</h1></header><!-- .entry-header -->
<div class="entry-content">
<figure id="attachment_515" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-515" style="width: 300px" class="wp-caption alignright"><a href="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Bifrost1.jpg"><img decoding="async" src="http://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Bifrost1-300x201.jpg" alt="" title="Bifrost" width="300" height="201" class="size-medium wp-image-515" srcset="https://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Bifrost1-300x201.jpg 300w, https://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Bifrost1-446x300.jpg 446w, https://norse-mythology.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/Bifrost1.jpg 584w" sizes="(max-width: 300px) 100vw, 300px" /></a><figcaption id="caption-attachment-515" class="wp-caption-text">The Rainbow Bridge between Asgard and Midgard in Richard Wagner&#8217;s <em>Das Rheingold</em>, directed by Otto Schenk (1990)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Asgard (<a href="http://norse-mythology.org/learn-old-norse/">Old Norse</a> <em>Ásgarðr</em>, &#8220;Enclosure of the <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/the-aesir-gods-and-goddesses/">Aesir</a>) is one of the <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/cosmology/the-nine-worlds/">Nine Worlds</a> of Norse mythology and the home and fortress of the Aesir, one of the two tribes of gods (the other being the <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/the-vanir-gods-and-goddesses/">Vanir</a>, who have their home in <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/cosmology/the-nine-worlds/vanaheim/">Vanaheim</a>). Asgard is located in the sky<sup>[1]</sup> (albeit spiritually rather than physically, of course) and is connected to <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/cosmology/the-nine-worlds/midgard/">Midgard</a>, the world of humanity, by the rainbow bridge <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/cosmology/bifrost/">Bifrost</a>.</p>
<p>The <em>-gard</em> element in Asgard&#8217;s name is a reference to the ancient Germanic concept of the distinction between the <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/concepts/innangard-and-utangard/">innangard and utangard</a>. That which is innangard (“inside the fence”) is orderly, law-abiding, and civilized, while that which is utangard (“beyond the fence”) is chaotic, anarchic, and wild. This applies both to the geographical plane and the human psyche; thoughts and actions can be innangard or utangard just as readily as spatial locations. Asgard is the ultimate model of the innangard, while <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/cosmology/the-nine-worlds/jotunheim/">Jotunheim</a>, the “Homeland of the <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/gods-and-creatures/giants/">Giants</a>,” is the epitome of the utangard.</p>
<p>Midgard (&#8220;Middle Enclosure&#8221;), the world of human civilization, is, as the name implies, somewhere in the middle &#8211; not quite as innangard as Asgard and not quite as utangard as Jotunheim. But Midgard is a space enclosed, on the geographical plane, by fences, and on the psychological plane by norms and laws. This makes it much closer &#8211; at least in theory &#8211; to Asgard than to Jotunheim. In other words, Asgard is the divine model upon which the pre-Christian Norse people patterned their world.</p>
<p>Looking for more great information on Norse mythology and religion? While this site provides the ultimate <em>online</em> introduction to the topic, my book <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/viking-spirit-introduction-norse-mythology-religion/"><em>The Viking Spirit</em></a> provides the ultimate introduction to Norse mythology and religion <em>period</em>. I’ve also written a popular list of <a href="http://norse-mythology.org/the-10-best-norse-mythology-books/">The 10 Best Norse Mythology Books</a>, which you’ll probably find helpful in your pursuit. </p>
